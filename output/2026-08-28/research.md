## U.S. national news

1. The U.S. Bureau of Labor Statistics released its Employment Projections: 2025-2035 Summary on August 27, 2026. BLS said the U.S. economy is projected to add 5.9 million jobs from 2025 to 2035, with private healthcare and social assistance projected to add the most jobs, more than 2.2 million, and utilities projected as the fastest-growing major sector at 9.8%. BLS also said nearly all projected utilities growth is tied to electric power generation, transmission, and distribution because of increasing electricity demand, including artificial-intelligence-related demand. Practical consequence: this is a useful “where work is moving” story for listeners thinking about midlife job stability, caregiving, retraining, or the pressure that AI and energy demand are already putting on the economy.
2. BLS also released its Displaced Workers Summary on August 27, 2026. It reported that from January 2023 through December 2025, 3.3 million workers were displaced from jobs they had held for at least 3 years, up by 746,000 from the prior survey period. In January 2026, 66.1% of those long-tenured displaced workers were reemployed, but the reemployment rate for workers ages 55 to 64 was lower at 57.3%. Practical consequence: this is one of the clearest current official indicators that older workers remain more vulnerable when jobs disappear, even when the broader labor market sounds stable.
3. The FDA announced on August 27, 2026 that it approved Lisraya (brepocitinib) tablets for dermatomyositis in adults, calling it the first FDA-approved oral treatment option for that disease. FDA said the phase 3 study included 241 adults and found higher average improvement scores with Lisraya 30 mg versus placebo, while the drug carries boxed warnings including serious infections, malignancies, major adverse cardiovascular events, and thrombosis. Practical consequence: the story is not “miracle cure,” but a concrete new treatment option with important safety context.

Practical consequence: it is a useful culture-and-local-life story because it treats Kyoto not just as a temple destination but as a place connected by food routes, landscape, and active travel.
