## U.S. national news

1. The U.S. Bureau of Labor Statistics said on August 28, 2026 that the preliminary benchmark revision to March 2026 total nonfarm employment was -79,000, or -0.1%. Practical consequence: the revision is small in percentage terms, but it tells listeners the labor market headline can still be softer under the surface than the first estimate suggests.
2. BLS also released its County Employment and Wages Summary on August 28, 2026. It said employment increased in 151 of the 376 largest U.S. counties from March 2025 to March 2026, national employment rose to 154.8 million, and the nation’s average weekly wage in the first quarter of 2026 rose to $1,654, up 3.9% over the year. Practical consequence: this is a grounded snapshot of where pay is still rising and where job growth is uneven, which matters more to daily life than a single stock-market mood line.
3. The FDA announced on August 28, 2026 that it approved Mimrylo (rusfertide) for adults with polycythemia vera. FDA said it is the first approved treatment of its kind, that the phase 3 VERIFY trial enrolled 293 adults, and that 76.9% of patients on Mimrylo required no phlebotomies during the 32-week study period versus 32.9% on placebo. Practical consequence: this is a concrete treatment advance, but it should be framed as a new option for a serious blood disorder rather than as a general “breakthrough” story.

## Virginia Beach

1. Virginia Beach’s August 19 West Nile virus notice was updated on August 28, 2026 to say the Virginia Department of Health had confirmed two human cases of West Nile virus in Virginia Beach. The city said mosquito control efforts had been increased. Practical consequence: for listeners over 50, this is a practical weekend planning item, especially for dawn and dusk outdoor time.
2. Virginia Beach’s Special Event Info page lists Funk Fest Beach Party for August 28-29 and the East Coast Surfing Championships for August 28-30. The city says there are no planned road closures for the weekend, but heavy traffic is expected between 25th-29th streets on Atlantic Avenue for Funk Fest and between Rudee Loop and 5th Street on Atlantic Avenue for ECSC, while the 2nd Street beach access will be closed for event use. Practical consequence: this is mostly a timing and parking story, not a safety alarm.
3. The city’s ReadyVB post highlighted hurricane-season readiness guidance, including a 72-hour emergency kit and VBAlert sign-up. The post itself is not new today, but it remains current city guidance as the season continues through November 30. Practical consequence: it is a useful “do one small thing this weekend” item rather than a sign of an active storm threat.

Kyoto City’s disaster portal checked on August 29, 2026 showed Level 2 heavy-rain and landslide advisories, plus a thunder advisory, while also showing no current rail operating issues as of the portal update. Practical consequence: the morning message is not “stay home,” but “check conditions once before you go and keep plans flexible.”
2. Kyoto City posted on August 28, 2026 that applications had opened for its 令和8年度 京都市AI・ロボティクス等導入検討 伴走支援事業, which offers expert-backed support for small and midsize businesses considering AI or robotics adoption. Practical consequence: this is a useful local-economy signal that Kyoto is trying to turn AI from a buzzword into hands-on support for smaller employers.
3. Kyoto Prefecture’s August 28, 2026 governor press conference announced 京都ジョブフェア2026 for October 16-17 at Kyoto産業会館ホール. The prefecture said day one will be 留学生DAY and day two will include all job seekers, including seminars aimed at middle and senior generations. Practical consequence: the labor story in Kyoto is not only about tourism; it is also about who can keep working, retrain, or re-enter.

2. Office on Women’s Health guidance checked on August 29, 2026 continues to describe common menopause symptoms including hot flashes, sleep problems, and mood changes, and points listeners toward clinician-supported treatment planning when symptoms are disruptive.
3. NCCIH continues to say that only a small number of studies have examined detoxes and cleanses in people, that claimed benefits rely on weak evidence, and that some detoxes and cleanses can cause harm including dehydration and electrolyte problems.
4. Evidence-safe framing to preserve in the script: the liver, kidneys, lungs, skin, and gastrointestinal tract already carry out the body’s normal waste-processing functions.
5. Safe small action for today: write down one symptom, one sleep detail, and one possible trigger or pattern before trying to “reset” the body with a cleanse.
