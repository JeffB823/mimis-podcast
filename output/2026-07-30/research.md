## U.S. national news

1. The Federal Reserve said on July 29, 2026 that it kept the federal funds target range at 3.5% to 3.75%. The official statement said economic activity is still expanding, but uncertainty remains elevated partly because of the Middle East conflict, and inflation is still above target with energy among the affected sectors. Practical consequence: borrowing costs stay high, and the Fed is explicitly signaling that energy-driven price pressure is still part of the inflation story.
2. The same July 29 Federal Reserve statement showed a 9-3 vote, with Beth Hammack, Neel Kashkari, and Lorie Logan preferring a quarter-point increase. Practical consequence: even without a hike, the internal split tells listeners that rates may stay restrictive for longer if inflation does not cool.
3. AP reported on July 29, 2026 that the U.S. Senate advanced a Russia sanctions bill by an 86-12 procedural vote while Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelenskyy watched from the gallery. Practical consequence: this remains foreign-policy news, but the bill targets buyers of Russian energy and sits inside a broader energy-price environment that is already affecting household costs.
4. AP also reported on July 29, 2026 that the Conference Board's consumer-confidence index fell to 90.8 in July from 92.2 in June, with gas-price pressure tied to the renewed U.S.-Iran conflict weighing on sentiment. Practical consequence: listeners do not need to treat this as abstract macroeconomics; it is a sign that fuel and grocery anxiety is already shaping how households feel.

## Mature women's health

1. I did not verify a major new menopause or detox/cleanse clinical development published in the last 24 hours that met this show's sourcing bar.
2. NCCIH continues to state that only a small number of studies on detoxification programs in people exist, and that favorable findings have generally come from low-quality studies.
3. NCCIH also continues to warn that some detox or fasting-style approaches can cause problems such as dehydration, weakness, fainting, and other harms depending on the method used.
4. The Office on Women's Health continues to advise that bothersome menopause symptoms are worth discussing with a health care provider, and it specifically suggests tracking triggers or symptoms such as hot flashes.
5. Practical consequence: today's evidence-safe action should stay modest. A good choice is to write down one symptom and when it tends to appear, and to avoid buying a cleanse product before checking medication overlap and basic safety.
6. It remains accurate to remind listeners that the body already has built-in waste-processing systems, including the kidneys and liver, and that sweeping "detox" claims need more evidence than marketing language.
